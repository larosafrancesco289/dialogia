# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Dialogia is for privacy-conscious learners and AI power users who want a flexible model workspace
without losing the calm, structured experience of a good tutor. The primary user is learning,
thinking, researching, or testing ideas with AI and cares about improving over time, not just
getting one-off answers.

The product is intentionally more capable than a generic chat app. Users can choose models, use web
search, inspect reasoning and metrics, attach rich inputs, and work locally with their own history.
Those controls serve users who like AI and want agency, but they must never make the interface feel
like a cockpit.

## Product Purpose

Dialogia exists to make AI help people get better: clearer thinkers, stronger learners, better
question askers, and more reflective users of models. Tutor Mode is the center of gravity. It should
feel like a serious learning companion with memory, structure, progress, diagnostic questions, and
adaptive pacing.

The flexible chat surface still matters. Dialogia should let users bring their favorite models,
provider routes, search tools, attachments, and reasoning controls into one local-first workspace.
Success means the power is available when needed and invisible when not needed.

## Brand Personality

Dialogia should feel scholarly, calm, capable, and humane. It has the confidence of a well-kept
study desk rather than the loud confidence of a SaaS dashboard. It should be pleasant to return to,
easy to trust, and respectful of attention.

Three words: learned, unobtrusive, encouraging.

The experience should get out of the user's way while making progress feel tangible. It should be
nice to use in the ordinary sense: thoughtful copy, clear hierarchy, low friction, and no visual
noise pretending to be intelligence.

## Anti-references

Dialogia must not feel generic. It should not look like a default AI chat wrapper, a purple-gradient
startup tool, a neon/cyberpunk console, a glassmorphism demo, or a bloated productivity cockpit.

Avoid anything that turns tutor mode into gamified noise. Avoid dashboards that make learning feel
like analytics theater. Avoid visual patterns that imply power through clutter: too many panels, too
many badges, dense controls with equal weight, or decorative metrics.

## Design Principles

1. Make the learner better, not merely answered. Tutor interactions should reveal progress,
   misconceptions, next steps, and confidence without becoming bureaucratic.
2. Keep power close but quiet. Advanced model controls, search, reasoning, privacy, and metrics
   should be discoverable, reversible, and visually secondary until needed.
3. Preserve intellectual calm. The interface should support reading, thinking, and revision for long
   sessions without visual fatigue.
4. Prefer guidance over spectacle. Motion, color, and components should clarify state and attention,
   not perform intelligence.
5. Be local-first and trust-preserving. Privacy posture should be visible where it matters and calm
   everywhere else.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for contrast, keyboard access, focus visibility, and readable type. All core tutor
and chat workflows should remain usable with reduced motion, without relying on color alone, and on
mobile touch targets of at least 44px where practical.

Tutor feedback should be supportive and precise. The product should interpret user input
generously, especially when dictated through speech-to-text, and should avoid punishing spelling,
phrasing, or uncertainty.
