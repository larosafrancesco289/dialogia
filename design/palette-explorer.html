Dialogia · Design System · Palette Study

Light parchment, dark ink.

A side-by-side study for re-introducing dark mode. The light theme keeps its parchment-and-gold identity; the dark theme is rebuilt from first principles so it reads as tasteful rather than costume. Two dark candidates below — pick one.

00

The idea, and what went wrong last time

The brand thread is gold as a precious metal — used sparingly so it reads as foil, never as a yellow fill. Light mode is parchment + gold; dark mode is ink + purple, with the same gold as the shared accent. The old dark mode failed for three concrete reasons, all fixable:

Cause 1

Cool blue contamination

The reasoning / thinking panels introduced rgba(93,123,255) and rgba(94,153,255) — cold blues dropped into a warm gold+purple palette. That single clash is what made it feel incoherent. Fixed: reasoning chips now stay in the purple family.

Cause 2

Near-black canvas

The old canvas was #0a0908 — almost pure black. You can't show elevation on black, and it makes gold "glare". Material's guidance: start at a dark grey (~#121212) and lift surfaces with lightness, not shadow.

Cause 3

Saturated accents that vibrate

Full-strength gold/purple "buzz" against dark backgrounds. The fix is the Material rule: desaturate and lighten accents for dark mode (~the 200 tone) so they sit calmly and still pass contrast.

Principle

Gold is rationed

In both themes gold only appears on one or two elements per view — the primary action, a hairline rule, a focus ring. Restraint is what separates "luxury" from "Vegas".

Why these colours

Warm neutrals, not white. The canvas #f4f1ec and surface #fdfbf8 carry a faint warm cast so the page feels like aged paper, not a clinical screen. The slight canvas→surface step gives cards a quiet lift without shadows doing all the work.

Gold earns its place. #b9975b is an antique, slightly desaturated gold — closer to brass than to highlighter yellow. It appears only on the send button and the active rail marker, so it reads as a metal detail. Purple #6d2a8a is the foil to gold: deep, regal, and used only for emphasis and links — never as a fill.

Dark A — Candlelit Study

The conservative, "obviously a sibling of light" option. The base is a warm charcoal (brown-black, like the light theme's parchment dimmed to embers). Gold stays the hero; purple appears only as a soft accent on reasoning and emphasis. This is the safest reintroduction — hard to get wrong, easy to love.

Why these colours

Warm, not black. Canvas #16140f is a brown-tinted charcoal — the same hue family as the light parchment, just dimmed. Avoiding pure black (the old #0a0908) is what lets the three surface steps (#16140f → #1e1b16 → #262119) read as gentle elevation instead of flat voids.

Accents lifted, not amped. Gold moves to #cda85f and purple to a lavender #b48ad0 — both lighter and slightly desaturated versus light mode, following Material's "use the 200 tone in dark" rule so they glow softly rather than vibrate. Text is off-white #ece6db, never #ffffff, to cut glare.

STRENGTHS
Unmistakably the same brand as light. Lowest risk of looking "costume". Gold reads as candle-glow, which suits the archive metaphor perfectly.
TRADE-OFF
Purple is quiet here — if you want dark mode to feel distinctly "purple" as its identity, this leans more brown than violet. That's what Dark B is for.
text on canvas ≈ 13.8:1 AAA muted on canvas ≈ 6.5:1 AA+ gold on surface ≈ 7.6:1 AAA purple on surface ≈ 6.1:1 AA
03

Dark B — Imperial Twilight

The bolder option, and the one that delivers "dark and purple" literally. The base is a deep aubergine ink — a heavily desaturated violet-charcoal — so purple is the air the whole UI breathes, not a sticker on top. Gold becomes the rare warm spark against the cool dark. This is more distinctive, with slightly more care needed to keep it from tipping over the top.

Why these colours

Purple as a hue, not a sticker. The base #141021 is a violet so desaturated it almost reads neutral — but every surface inherits that undertone, so the lavender accents feel native rather than pasted on. This is the literal "dark and purple" direction, executed with restraint via desaturation.

Gold becomes precious. Against a cool ground, the warm gold #d4b06a stands out far more, so it's used even more sparingly — the single send button, nothing else. Complementary warm-on-cool is what gives this option its drama; the danger is doing it too much, which the rationing prevents.

STRENGTHS
Most distinctive and memorable. Delivers the purple identity you asked for. Warm-gold-on-cool-ink is a genuinely elegant, "after-hours" mood.
TRADE-OFF
Further from the light theme's warmth — the two modes feel like cousins, not twins. Purple tints need discipline app-wide or it can drift toward "gamer". More QA across every surface.
text on canvas ≈ 14:1 AAA muted on canvas ≈ 6.4:1 AA+ gold on surface ≈ 8.2:1 AAA lavender on surface ≈ 7:1 AAA
04

How to choose

Both pass WCAG AA (most pairs hit AAA) and both fix all three failure causes from the old theme. The decision is purely about identity:

Pick A if…

Cohesion matters most

You want dark mode to be unmistakably the same product as light, you reuse this system across many projects, and you'd rather "safe and beautiful" than "bold". Lowest maintenance. This is my recommendation as the default.

Pick B if…

Identity matters most

You want the dark theme to have its own strong character — the "dark and purple" you described — and you're willing to keep purple tints disciplined across every surface to keep it tasteful.

Once you choose, I'll wire the winning set into styles/tokens.css under a .dark / [data-theme] scope, restore the theme toggle that was removed in 9344b5d, and — critically — re-skin the reasoning/thinking panels in the chosen accent so the old blue clash never returns.
